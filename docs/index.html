How it works

Speak! lives in the menu bar and stays out of sight until you hold the key.

  1. Hold Right ⌥

    Or Right ⌘, or any key you set. Short taps are ignored, so your ⌥-shortcuts keep working.

  2. Speak

    A small pill shows your voice level and the time. Talk for a few words or five minutes.

  3. Release

    The text is typed into the app you're in: a terminal, an editor, a chat, a pull request comment.

Made for people who talk to AI all day

Prompts for coding agents are full of terms, file names and two languages at once. Speak! is tuned for exactly that.

Russian and English in one sentence

“Сделай rebase на main” comes out with rebase spelled the way you write it, not transliterated.

Your vocabulary, learned once

Select a misheard word in any app, right-click, choose Fix Spelling in Speak!, type it right. Every later dictation gets it right too.

Hears what you meant

With Parakeet a small extra model matches words that sound like your terms, so “пул реквист” still becomes pull request.

Leaves the clipboard alone

Text is typed straight into the focused field. Password fields are skipped, and nothing typed there is saved.

Never loses a dictation

If the text can't be inserted, it waits in the menu, one click from the clipboard. If recognition fails, the audio is kept for a retry.

Updates in one click

When a new version is out, the menu says so. Click Install: Speak! downloads it, checks the signature and restarts.

Questions

Does Speak! work offline?

Yes. The default engine, NVIDIA Parakeet, and the Whisper models run on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded. Only the optional Gemini engine sends audio to Google, on your own API key.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no subscription, no account and no word limit. The source code is on GitHub.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple Silicon on macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Why does macOS block the first launch?

Speak! is signed by its author, not notarized by Apple. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, find “Speak was blocked”, and click Open Anyway. You do this once.

What permissions does it need?

Microphone, to hear you while you hold the key. Accessibility, to type the text. Input Monitoring, to notice the hotkey. The welcome window checks them for you.

How is it different from macOS Dictation?

You hold a key instead of toggling a mode, it handles Russian and English in one sentence, it learns your terms, and it keeps every dictation in a history you can copy from.
